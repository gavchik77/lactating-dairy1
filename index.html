Dairy cow example

Propose a ration to cover the cow's needs

A group of 550 kg dairy cows are required to produce 25 L of milk/day containing 45 g/kg fat (4.5%) and 34 g/kg protein (3.4%).

The ration consists of:

  • Grass silage: 250 g/kg DM (25% DM), ME 11 MJ/kg DM, CP 12%, ration quality q = 0.5.
  • Dairy concentrate: 900 g/kg DM (90% DM), ME 13.0 MJ/kg DM, CP 18%, ration quality q = 0.7.

It is anticipated that the cow will consume 16.0 kg/day DM. Propose the ration to cover the cow's needs.

Your calculation should determine

  1. The target dietary CP and the proportion of silage and concentrate.
  2. The quality (q) of the mixed ration.
  3. Maintenance ME requirement.
  4. ME required for 25 L of milk at 4.5% fat and 3.4% protein.
  5. Total ME required per day.
  6. ME to be supplied by silage and concentrate.
  7. Dry matter intake from each feed and total DMI.
  8. Fresh/as-fed kilograms of silage and concentrate per cow per day.

Step 1 · Protein

Select the CP target, then balance the two feeds

The supplied worked example states that a lactating cow requires about 15–17% CP and uses 16% CP for this cow.

12(1 − x) + 18x = target CP, where x = concentrate fraction
Enter the target CP and the two DM proportions.

Step 2 · Ration quality

Calculate the q value of the mixed ration

q of ration = (silage fraction × 0.5) + (concentrate fraction × 0.7)
Calculate the weighted q first. The supplied worked example then uses approximately q = 0.65 for interpolation.

Step 3 · Maintenance energy

Calculate maintenance ME for a 550 kg cow

At 550 kg, Table 1 gives 58 MJ/day at q = 0.6 and 61 MJ/day at q = 0.7.

At q ≈ 0.65, use the midpoint between 58 and 61 MJ/day
No liveweight change is specified in the problem.

Step 4 · Milk production energy

Calculate the ME required for 25 L of milk

For milk containing 4.5% fat and 3.4% protein, Table 2 gives: 5.50 MJ/L at q = 0.6 and 5.21 MJ/L at q = 0.7.

At q ≈ 0.65: ME per L ≈ midpoint of 5.50 and 5.21
Milk ME/day = ME per L × 25 L
Use the table values and the cow's 25 L/day milk yield.

Step 5 · Total daily energy

Add maintenance, milk production and body-weight change

Total ME = maintenance ME + milk ME + body-weight-change ME
Worked-example target about 193.5 MJ/day
Add the three daily energy terms.

Step 6 · Divide the total ME between the feeds

Use the Step 1 ration proportions

Silage ME = total ME × silage fraction
Concentrate ME = total ME × concentrate fraction
The two energy contributions should add back to the total ME requirement.

Step 9 · Practical feeding check

Does the mathematically correct ration also follow practical dairy-feeding rules?

Rules to keep in mind

  • Dairy concentrate: use a high-energy, balanced dairy nut or meal containing about 14–18% CP.
  • With average-to-poor grass silage: a 16–18% CP concentrate is appropriate.
  • Minerals and vitamins: the concentrate should contain a balanced premix, including calcium, phosphorus, magnesium and vitamins A, D and E.
  • Early lactation: a practical concentrate allowance is commonly about 4–8 kg/cow/day, with roughly 7–8 kg/day treated here as an upper teaching benchmark to reduce digestive upset and forage substitution.
  • Mid-to-late lactation: concentrate allowance is normally reduced as milk yield falls and forage supplies more of the requirement.
